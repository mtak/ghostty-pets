# M2 — smart visibility feasibility

- **Status:** APPROVED by the user. Available Linux visibility cases pass; the limits below remain recorded.
- **Branch:** `codex/m2-visibility` in `.worktrees/m2`.
- **Scope:** stateless cursor proximity fade, coherent region text fading, conservative and cursor-only comparison builds, and a small Linux visual/performance probe. M1 files remain unchanged.

## Decision log

1. The approved M2 plan layers a visibility function over the M1 shader generator. It reuses the same PNG and packed pixels. M1 generated bytes and source code were not edited.
2. For this placeholder sprite, three source-X regions (tail, body, head) provide coherent localized fading. This is a proof layout; final scene regions will be defined with final assets.
3. A cursor overlapping or near a region reduces its opacity immediately. The previous cursor position restores over 0.80 s using `iPreviousCursor` and `iTimeCursorChange`; no retained frame state is used.
4. Conservative text mode takes at most 20 terminal-image samples (five rows × four columns) per affected region. Its outer rows sit close to both edges; the original centered 4 × 4 grid missed a thin bottom-edge red mark. One non-background hit fades that region to 8% sprite opacity and exits the loop early. This favors readable text at the cost of false positives. If cursor fade already protects a region, text probes are skipped.
5. Linux Ghostty's sampled screen color and `iBackgroundColor` did not compare directly on a blank surface. A diagnostic shader showed a visible `#1e1e2e` screen pixel and a much lighter output from the raw background uniform. Converting the uniform from sRGB to linear made a blank region match in this build. The behavior of other blending modes, especially macOS's default, remains unverified.
6. `cursor-only` removes the text-detection function and is built only for comparison. It is not an approved fallback for v1; if conservative detection proves unusable, ADR-0005 and FR-18 require human sign-off before downgrading.

## Checks and observations

**Environment:** Linux, Ghostty `1.3.0-dev+0000000`, GTK/OpenGL build, X11-backed test windows under a Wayland session. The EGL log reported AMD device authorization failures and fallback behavior; the actual renderer device was not measured. macOS is **NOT TESTED**.

- `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v`: **5 passed**, including M1 artifact equivalence and M2 artifact reproducibility. Source review confirmed that the cursor-only build omits the text-probe function.
- M2 shaders are standalone and deterministic. Revised conservative output is **5,846 bytes**, cursor-only **5,153 bytes**, versus **3,517 bytes** for M1 at either scale. The 32 × 24 PNG has 331 opaque pixels. At 2×, there are 1,324 opaque fragments and at most 26,480 additional text samples per rendered frame; outside or transparent pixels do none of this work.
- The installed Ghostty config documentation lists `iChannel0`, `iResolution`, `iCurrentCursor`, `iPreviousCursor`, `iTime`, `iTimeCursorChange`, and `iBackgroundColor`. A diagnostic shader visibly located the cursor rectangle and confirmed the Linux background-color conversion. M2 2× conservative and cursor-only variants loaded without shader compilation errors in the observed logs. 1× runtime compilation was not separately observed.
- In the overlapping dark-theme ANSI text fixture, M1 changed **85 of 581** reference glyph pixels by more than 16 RGB levels; M2 changed **0 of 581** by that amount. It still altered those 85 pixels slightly (at most 16 levels). The affected body region faded while the head remained visible. Outside the sprite, the captured terminal area matched shader-off exactly after excluding a transient window scrollbar outside the terminal image.
- A light-theme overlapping ANSI text capture showed the body region fading and the word remaining legible. This is visual evidence, not a pixel-mask comparison against a light-theme shader-off capture.
- A bottom-right cursor capture showed the body region fading while the head remained visible. In a focused-window cursor-move capture, one body pixel changed from its faded value `(79, 67, 61)` through `(153, 127, 97)` and `(221, 183, 135)` before reaching full sprite color `(236, 195, 143)`. Cursor blinking caused separate bright/dark alternations before the move. These captures support smooth restoration when frames are rendered; redraw timing may vary on unfocused surfaces.
- Separate five-second process samples before the edge-probe revision measured **56.31 CPU seconds for M1** and **56.70 for M2**, with RSS **1,015.2 MB** and **1,016.9 MB** respectively. A later sample of revised M2 measured **13.09 CPU seconds**, indicating substantial focus/load variability. These are not reliable estimates of the incremental CPU cost, and GPU or battery cost was not measured. No obvious responsiveness issue was observed in the captured static cases.

## Additional user validation and remaining limits

### User validation after the initial report

On 29 October 2026, the user reported that the shader loads, cursor proximity fades the pet, and a TUI makes it fade. In the user's [red foreground screenshot](evidence/m2/user-red-text-no-fade.png), text overlaps a bright sprite without the expected region fade; in the [blue background screenshot](evidence/m2/user-blue-background-fade.png), the sprite fades.

The screenshots suggest a fractional display scale: the sprite's apparent width and inset are larger than their shader-pixel dimensions. Under that inferred scale, the red glyph strokes sit below the old grid's lowest sample row, while the blue background extends into it. A Ghostty shader-chain regression fixture in [m2-red-edge.glsl](../tests/fixtures/m2-red-edge.glsl) placed a thin red mark at that edge. The prior shader left **176 full-tan body pixels** in the captured region; the revised shader left **zero**, fading the body. This verifies the edge-probe change on the available Linux Ghostty build.

The user then supplied a [new red-text capture](evidence/m2/user-red-text-fades-after-fix.png) and reported that the corgi fades. Pixel comparison against the [earlier red-text capture](evidence/m2/user-red-text-no-fade.png) found **996 changed pixels**, all within `x=172–205, y=97–130` of the 250 × 147 screenshots. Full-color tan pixels in the lower-right crop fell from **444 to 240**, with **196** pixels at the faded-tan color. This supports a localized body fade for the original red-text case.

The user confirmed the active path was `dist/m2-corgi-2x-conservative.glsl` in the M2 worktree and supplied a [selection capture](evidence/m2/user-selected-text-fades.png). The selected letters `TEXT` are visually readable, and the pet is faded over the selection. This is a user-reported pass with screenshot evidence; the user's Ghostty version and renderer were not supplied.

The text detector is an image heuristic. It cannot identify semantic glyphs, and colored backgrounds, gradients, selections, and graphics may cause false positives; foreground colors similar to the background may cause false negatives. Its fixed grid can miss thin or isolated glyphs between sample points. The 0.02 linear-color threshold and the one-hit rule were tested only in the cases above.

- **Selection:** PASS in the user's screenshot. The local X11 automation attempt did not create a selection, so this result rests on the user's manual test and capture.
- **Full-screen TUI overlap:** The user reports that a TUI makes the pet fade, but its exact content and overlap were not supplied. In the local Vim capture, the status line sat above the sprite, so that capture verifies loading in a TUI, not text protection under one.
- **Dense output, light-theme baseline comparison, 1× runtime rendering, rapid cursor movements, narrow panes, HiDPI, GPU/battery impact:** NOT TESTED.
- **macOS/Metal:** NOT TESTED. The same shader may need a color-space adjustment because Ghostty's blending default differs; do not infer compatibility or text-detection accuracy from Linux.

### Exact human checklist for the remaining cases

For each available Linux or macOS system, record OS, Ghostty version, backend/GPU, display scale, shader checksum, theme, and screenshots or a short clip. Use the conservative M2 file with an absolute `custom-shader` path and `custom-shader-animation = true`; compare each case to shader-off and M1 with the same terminal content.

1. On both dark and light themes, position the text cursor inside and next to each sprite region. Move it away, pause, and confirm only nearby regions fade, the new cursor area clears promptly, and the old area restores smoothly. Repeat with rapid moves.
2. Place plain text, ANSI-colored text, and dense output directly under the sprite at 1× and 2×. Confirm every covered glyph is readable. Note missed glyphs, excessive fading, and abrupt region boundaries.
3. Select text under and beside the sprite. Check both selection foreground and background; note false positives and whether glyphs remain readable.
4. Open Vim/Neovim or another full-screen TUI and arrange real text or a status line under the sprite. Repeat with inverse-video blocks and colored backgrounds.
5. Resize to a narrow/short pane. Confirm the shader has no clipping, compile errors, or visible changes outside the sprite; record any small-pane failure for M5.
6. Remove the `custom-shader` line and reload/restart Ghostty. Confirm the unchanged terminal returns. If the window becomes unusable, remove that line outside Ghostty and restart.

## Gate and unresolved questions

**Verified:** Linux dark-theme overlap protection for one ANSI fixture, localized cursor and body-region fading, smooth restoration on observed frames, M1 preservation, deterministic standalone builds, and bounded additional sampling. The user's captures additionally show red-text and selected-text protection with the conservative shader.

**Inferred:** the fixed sample bound limits cost for this tiny sprite. The process samples varied too much to establish an incremental CPU cost, and they do not establish performance for a full backyard or on a hardware GPU.

**Untested:** a directly observed TUI overlap, the other visual cases and platforms listed above, and reliable GPU cost. The exact cross-platform background color-space policy remains open; no substantive ADR change has been made.

**Gate: M2 approved by the user with the stated limits. M3 has not started and requires its own implementation plan and approval.**
